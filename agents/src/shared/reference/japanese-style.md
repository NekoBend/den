# Japanese style

Rules for a document written in Japanese,
used by documenter's guide mode and by its translate mode into Japanese.
A style guide the user names wins.
Otherwise apply these rules to the whole document:
notation follows the JTF Japanese Standard Style Guide (version 4.0),
and sentences follow 公用文作成の考え方 (Agency for Cultural Affairs, 2022).

## Register

- Write the body in です・ます from start to end,
  whatever the kind of document,
  unless the user asks for another style.
  A quotation may keep its own style.
- Write headings as a noun phrase or in plain form,
  without 「。」 at the end,
  and give every heading the same form.
- In one bulleted list, end every item the same way,
  and either end every item with 「。」 or none of them.
- Keep politeness moderate:
  do not use ございます, do not stack polite forms,
  and use おります and いたします sparingly.
  Keep forms such as ご確認ください for a few requests,
  and use してください for the rest.

## Sentences

- When a sentence reaches about 50 to 60 characters,
  check whether it can be split;
  the number is a prompt to look, not a limit.
- Make one point per sentence.
  End the sentence instead of chaining clauses with the particle が
  or with the continuative form.
- Do not repeat the same particle, such as の, に, or も,
  several times in a row.
- Make it clear who does what,
  and keep the same subject to the end of the sentence.
- Put a condition at the start of the sentence.
- Place 読点 so that the sentence can be read only one way.

## Kanji and kana

- Use kanji within the 常用漢字表.
- Write these in hiragana:
  こと, とき, ところ, もの, ため, とおり, and よう
  when they have no concrete meaning;
  auxiliary verbs such as 〜ていく, 〜てくる, 〜てみる, 〜てほしい, and 〜ていただく;
  most conjunctions, such as さらに, しかし, したがって, ただし, and また;
  and いる, できる, ある, and ない.

## Numbers

- In horizontal text, use half-width Arabic numerals,
  with a comma every three digits: 12,500.
- Use 万, 億, and 兆 as kanji in large numbers: 1億2,644万.
- Use kanji numerals for approximate numbers and fixed words:
  数十人, 一部, 一時的, 第三者.
- Write か月 and か所 with a hiragana か.
- Do not use full-width digits.

## Characters, spaces, and symbols

- Write kanji, hiragana, and katakana in full width,
  and Arabic numerals and Latin letters in half width.
  Do not use half-width katakana.
- Put no space between a full-width and a half-width character:
  Windowsを再起動します.
- Use 「、」 and 「。」 as punctuation.
  End a sentence with 「。」 instead of ! or ?.
- Do not carry an English colon, semicolon, or dash into Japanese prose;
  rewrite the sentence instead.
- Use 「」 for quotations and references,
  『』 for titles of works,
  and full-width （） for supplementary notes.
- Write a range with the full-width wave sign in a document: 18～22歳.
  In a software string, write から instead.

## Katakana words

- Keep the final long vowel of a word that ends in -er, -or, or -ar:
  ユーザー, コンピューター, エレベーター.
- Also write the long vowel for -y, -ry, and -ty:
  メモリー, カテゴリー, コミュニティー,
  unless the product's glossary writes the word otherwise.
- Write a katakana compound without a separator,
  and add 「・」 only where a reader could split it wrongly.
- Choose the word the reader already uses,
  whether it is a kanji term or a loanword.
  When the exact meaning of a term does not matter in the sentence,
  use the more familiar word:
  ボトルネック rather than 律速 for engineers,
  議題 rather than アジェンダ for a general reader.
  When the exact meaning matters, keep the precise term,
  and explain it briefly at its first use.

## Units

- Write unit symbols as in SI, such as kg, MB, and Hz,
  with no space after the number: 2kg, 512MB, 30%.
- Write units of time in kanji: 時間, 分, 秒.
